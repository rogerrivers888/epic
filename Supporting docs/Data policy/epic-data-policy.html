Epic Data Policy

19 September 2026 · revised after experiments 1–4 · supersedes the Claude Doc draft of the same name

Google is the spine everywhere; nothing rented is stored; knowing what exists is free and permanent; ranking is done from what we own; we buy only for what is shown. Every rule below is one of those five applied.

Corrections (1 Oct 2026). Fixes for internal consistency: (1) ranking is done on the Google search response — one search per category, no per-ID Place Details calls to surface an owned place Google did not return — and the "rank the top ten from the census" instruction is withdrawn; (2) the OSM "Not on Google" match fence is 400 m everywhere, matching matchFence, not 150 m; (3) the SL5 worked example counts the live TripAdvisor fetch in each drawer open (3.2p an open, 9.6p for three, ~69.6p for a fresh search); (4) Google ratings are never stored beyond the 12 h search pool — only the derived, dated epic_score is kept, never Google's raw rating (the "ratings 7 d" cache is removed); (5) a maxed-out category pages with Google's page token only once a household goes past the 20, never by tiling another ring slice; (6) "Google is the only source a household sees" is scoped to discovery results, with the drawer's live, attributed TripAdvisor rating named as the exception. The rules

  1. Google is called on every search, and in discovery results Google is the only source a household sees in V1 — the one exception is an opened place's drawer, which also shows TripAdvisor's rating and reviews, fetched live and shown with attribution (the TripAdvisor rule below). Photographs and ratings have no substitute. Google's search results already carry rating, review count, price band, hours, website and photo references for up to 20 places per request, so a search-shaped call is the unit of purchase.
  2. Knowing what exists is free and permanent. The first search of an area, by anyone on any surface, leaves a census: every place ID Google returned, with our category and subcategory (from the query), the slice it came from and its rank, written to place_index with no cap. From then on the area's count is known and shown in the back office without calling Google. The census uses Google's IDs Only field mask, which is free with no cap; location and types are Pro-tier on Text Search and arrive the first time a place is bought for display. The census refreshes every 30 days or when the taxonomy changes.
  3. A place is a place ID. The Google place ID is the only Google content stored indefinitely. Coordinates may be held 30 days. Names, ratings, reviews, hours and photos live in memory for hours and are never written to the database or a device. Everything keyed on a place, reachability included, keys on the ID and coordinates, not a postcode.
  4. Prefer search over detail. A Text Search returns 20 places with ratings for one request price (0.16p a place). A Place Details call is one place for 2p. Never buy detail for a place a search could have covered.
  5. Rank the search response. One Text Search per category returns 20 places; order those by the Epic score (section Owning the score) and Google's rank, and show the top 10. Google's search decides which places appear — an owned place Google did not return is not fetched to force it in, because that would be a per-ID Place Details call. One search per category, never one per place.
  6. Photos are fetched for tiles on screen, a row at a time, never for a result set.
  7. TripAdvisor is drawer-only. Fetched live when a household opens a place and shown beside Google with its logo; never a discovery source and never an input to the Epic score. Experiment 3 (19 September) settled it: a 33% match on what Epic shows, a quarter of Google's review depth, no family signal, and blending demoted the attractions a family day out is built on.
  8. Free data is a check on Google, not a second source of discovery or opinion. OSM and the FSA register give the count the census is measured against. Every OSM place is looked up on Google by name (free); what Google returns is Google's, what it doesn't is a "Not on Google" residual shown only in the back office. If the credible residual is under 5% of visitable places, free data never surfaces new places in the app. This does not touch the owned layer: place_records remains what a device holds offline, menu links from venue pages stay in scope, and owned facts may fill any field Google leaves empty on a place page.
  9. No paid research per place in V1. Buying nine Google results and researching the tenth saves nothing.
  10. No new data source until the Places tab shows a material, reliable, cleansable gap. Google-first is what lets Epic open a new country by changing a bounding box.

Decision to revisit — rented photo bytes cached 10 hours in the browser (1 Oct 2026). A Google Place Photo is streamed through /api/photos/google and the response carries Cache-Control: private, max-age=36000 (EPIC_PHOTO_CACHE_SECONDS) — a per-device browser cache only, never server-stored, never a shared/edge cache, never redistributed. (The 10 hours is this browser cache; the server holds a photo in memory for only about an hour — a separate, shorter layer — so the "photos 1 h" in the implementation notes and this 10 h describe different caches and are not in conflict.) This is not justified by Google's 30-day caching allowance: that allowance is for latitude/longitude, not for photo content. It is kept for now as a performance measure (reopening the app within the day shows the photos it already had rather than re-buying every one), and flagged here as a decision to revisit against the Maps Platform terms for photo media — reduce or remove it if the terms do not permit a temporary byte cache. Lazy-loading (photos fetched only for tiles in or near the viewport, each Google photo fetched once a session) already removes the eager, result-set-wide buying this rule has always forbidden.

Owning the score

The Epic score is ours; the rating behind it is not. It is built from Google's rating, review count and rank as returned by the display search already bought, plus Epic's own signals (visits, household ratings, review recency), scaled to 0–100 and stamped with a date. It is a derived number, not a stored copy of Google's 4.6; the code already does this as crowd_band, and the score extends the same principle. It refreshes whenever a display search returns the place and the score is older than 90 days.

What the app shows

Every place the census knows about is showable, because names, ratings and photos are rented at the moment they are displayed, not owned. The list is bought, and the only number a household ever sees is the length of a list we can put in front of them.

No unbacked counts (owner, 20 Sep 2026). The census count was on Inspire as a free headline and it said 23 over a ring holding three to five. It was wrong in five directions at once and no wording fixed it: it summed whole postcode districts rather than the ring; it summed drawers, so one place could count twice; most of the ring had never been censused; a saturated slice makes it a floor; and it is only ever as of the last run. So: no household-facing number unless we can show the places behind it. Where we cannot produce a list, show no number. The census keeps its job — it is what the back office counts with and what decides where to collect next — and it is not a number a household is shown.

The once-a-day-per-area idea

Partly true. Google's service terms permit place IDs indefinitely and coordinates for 30 days; everything else is "no pre-fetching, caching or storing". Serving household B a rating bought for household A that morning is caching by that definition. The 12-hour in-memory pool is tolerated practice as a working set, not a right; keep it in memory, keep it short, and don't let the subscription price depend on it. A deploy emptying it is a warm-up cost of one 32p search per district. What is legitimately free after the first search: knowing what exists (the census), ranking (the Epic score), and re-buying cheaply (20 places per 3.2p search rather than 2p per place).

The census and the Places back office

Google cannot count. Every query returns its top 20 and pages to a hard stop at 60. A count is built by slicing: one query per Google place type inside each subcategory, restricted to the box; a slice returning a full 60 is saturated and split into four tiles or finer types until every slice returns fewer than 60. The union of IDs is the census. With one query per Google type inside 46 subcategories, SL5 is about 400 requests and a 30-minute ring about 69,000; at the IDs Only mask both are £0, so the request count is a pacing question, not a cost one.

Per place the census stores: place ID, the subcategory query it surfaced under, rank, slice, seen date. Coordinates (30 days) and Google types are added the first time a display search returns the place; where the by-name lookup matches an OSM place, OSM's coordinates may be stored as a fallback. Category and subcategory are a mapping table from Google types and queries to Epic's taxonomy; a taxonomy change re-maps for free and runs new slices only for new subcategories. OSM and, for food, the FSA register give the count the census is measured against: a subcategory well below either has a query gap, fixed by widening the fan-out at no cost.

Does Google give us everything?

Yes. All four experiments ran on 19 September for $4.59 in total, and the census itself cost nothing. The 53 "open map only" places from the earlier diff were a fence problem: OSM marks the centre of a golf course, Google marks the clubhouse, and a 150 m match radius called Wentworth, Ascot Racecourse and Swinley Forest "not on Google". The match fence is now 400 m, or a polygon's own extent plus 100 m, everywhere.

Now and later

V1, any country. Google for everything a household sees; the free census; the Epic score built from Google plus Epic's own signals; opinion and photos bought per screen; TripAdvisor live in the drawer only; first-party visits and ratings. OSM and FHRS in the back office only. No paid research per place, no country-specific sets in the app.

V2 trigger. A thin-page rate above ~15% on what households open, or a credible "Not on Google" residual above 5% in a district. Only then do the Claude pass and country-specific facts earn a build.

Open with TripAdvisor: whether a location ID may be stored under their standard terms, which decides if a drawer open is one call or two. Nothing else.

Next runs: the 30-minute ring around SL5 (172 outcodes, free) to check the total against the 2,571 previously held and to confirm the water park appears; SE1 as the density test; then the Places tab, deployed.

## Data policy (19 Sep 2026)

V1 is Google for everything a household sees. Rented vs owned is unchanged:
nothing from Google or TripAdvisor is written to the database or a device
except identifiers (Google place ID; TripAdvisor location ID only if the
terms allow) and lat/lng (30 days). The Epic score is ours.

The census (free, permanent, runs on every first search of an area)
- Trigger: ANY search of an area not censused in the last 30 days, from
  the app, the back office, or on demand; also a taxonomy change, for the
  affected subcategories only.
- Google cannot count. One Text Search per Google place type within each
  Epic subcategory, locationRestriction to the box, IDs Only field mask
  (free, no cap; anything more on Text Search bills at Pro or above and
  skuFor must say so). A slice returning 60 is saturated: split it (4
  tiles or finer types) until every slice returns < 60. Request count is
  a pacing question: keep a per-run ceiling for throughput and stay
  under Google's QPS.
- Write EVERY id seen to place_index: id, subcategory query, rank, slice,
  seen_at. No keep cap of any kind. lat/lng (expires 30 d) and google
  types are written the first time a display search returns the place;
  OSM coordinates may be stored as a fallback where the by-name lookup
  matched. The area board never needs coordinates.
- Category/subcategory come from a mapping table (google type + query ->
  Epic taxonomy). A taxonomy change re-maps for free.
- Cross-checks per box and subcategory, stored beside the census count:
  OSM count via Overpass; FHRS count via api.ratings.food.gov.uk (new
  adapter, OGL, free, daily) for food.
- Not on Google: for every OSM place in the box, one IDs Only Text Search
  by name within 400 m (the match fence, matching matchFence). Found -> record the mapping. Not found -> flag
  not_on_google with OSM's credibility signals.
- Retire the sweep's per-place Google research (own.seed / own.lead) as a
  discovery tool and the match-then-rate path (atlas.rating).
- Reachability and area pages key on place ID + lat/lng, not postcode.

The Epic score (owned)
- Inputs: Google rating + userRatingCount + rank from the display search
  already bought; Epic's own visits, household ratings, review recency.
  No TripAdvisor input (verdict tripadvisor.second-opinion, 19 Sep).
- Output: epic_score 0-100 per place ID with scored_at, plus a band. Never
  store Google's raw rating. Refresh from the next display search that
  returns the place when scored_at > 90 days.

Match fence (domain/matchFence.js, used by every by-name lookup)
- 400 m, or a polygon's half-extent + 100 m, capped at 5 km; boxes
  corrected by cos(latitude). Fail closed.

Search behaviour (app)
- The app reads the census index and the reachability ring. Names,
  ratings and photos are rented at display time; there is no "can show"
  subset. Every censused place is showable.
- Inspire: per category, the top 5 from one Enterprise+Atmosphere Text
  Search, locationRestriction = the ring's bounding box, results filtered
  to the ring's own sectors. No census count on a household screen.
  Rank the 20 returned by epic_score where present, Google order
  otherwise. Never call Place Details for a place a search returned.
- Category list: the 20 from that search. Only when a household goes past
  the 20 does the next page follow, by Google's page token (nextPageToken),
  the way the enrichment rule pages on demand. Never a further ring slice
  for a paid search (see "never tile for the paid search" below and the
  one-page-per-category cost model). Pool key: ring + category + page, 12 h.
- Retire Inspire's atlas and food-sweep pools and the minutes-to-km
  conversion; the atlas stays as a source of images and summaries only.
- Score any returned place with no epic_score from the search result.
- Photos only for tiles in the viewport, a row at a time.
- Drawer open: Google Place Details + TripAdvisor Location Details, live;
  TripAdvisor matched on latLong at the fence, fail closed; show both
  ratings side by side with logos and both review sets per the
  TripAdvisor display requirements. Purpose tripadvisor.drawer. If the
  terms allow, store the location ID after the first open; else look up
  each time. On 429, Google only.
- Wide areas: tile for the census (free), never tile for the paid search.
- OSM, FHRS, venue pages, Wikipedia and Wikidata are never presented to a
  household as a discovery or rating source in V1. The owned layer stays:
  place_records backs the device's offline store, menu links from venue
  pages remain in scope, and owned facts may fill fields Google leaves
  empty. No Claude research pass in V1.
- Drawer: render at once from epic_score + Google; the TripAdvisor panel
  fills when its call returns, never blocking. Offline, the drawer shows
  epic_score and owned facts only.

Places tab (back office)
- Input: postcode or outcode + reach (outcode | 30 min | 60 min).
- Area board: category -> subcategory rows with census count, OSM count,
  FHRS count (food), not-on-Google count, scored count, saturated slices,
  last census. Reads only place_index/OSM/FHRS. Cannot trigger a paid call.
- Click subcategory: run the exact app ranking and the same display
  search; show the top 10 as the app would. "Show another 10" = next
  page. Tiles fetch photos; opening a place fetches both details. All
  with purpose admin.explore.
- Not on Google drill-down with credibility signals and a free
  check-by-name action.
- Cost counter: this action / this session / today, live from
  provider_calls. Every paid control shows its price before the click.

Caches
- Search pool 12 h, photos 1 h, in memory, bounded, never persisted. A
  rented rating lives in the search pool and nowhere longer; only the
  derived, dated epic_score is kept, never Google's raw rating. Warm
  searches are served from the pool without a Google call. A deploy
  warming up is expected.

Ledger
- Every call to provider_calls with purpose, household, session, units,
  estimated cost. Purposes are stable strings; add, never rename.
- Log every search to searches with what was asked, shown and triggered.

First run

   not-on-Google, saturated slices, requests used, Google cost from the
   ledger (expect 0).
2. Widen to the 30-minute ring. Same report, per outcode and in total.
3. Experiments 1-4 are closed; their verdicts and re-open triggers live
   in data_verdicts. Next: the 30-minute ring, SE1 density test, then the
   Places tab deployed. Codex review before anything is called done.
